On-device AI experiments

Call notes, search and spam detection: a work in progress

+PLUS home How we test Which model fits Screening decisions Call notes Search SMS spam What's next Share data

Call notes and search in Phone+ come from the optional +PLUS AI plus-up: a language model that runs on the phone. This part is a work in progress. We are running experiments to see what we can get out of the open-weight Gemma 4 E2B model.

  • It may be possible to fit the larger Gemma 4 E4B on phones with more RAM.
  • Experiments are under way to find the best system prompts, to get acceptable results both with and without fine-tuning.
  • We are also evaluating on-phone spam detection for SMS messages, combining typical spam detection with classification by a fine-tuned language model.

More will be reported here in the future. If you have lots of classified sample calls or messages you are willing to share, please get in contact.

How we test

  • Everything stays local. The test data is a private set of real calls (about 110 recorded and transcribed calls) and texts (about 15,000 messages). It never goes to a network service, and only aggregate numbers appear here.
  • On the phone, and on a computer for comparison. On the phone the model runs in +PLUS AI (LiteRT-LM, int4, on the GPU of a Pixel 9). The same model also runs on a PC (llama.cpp), which is faster to experiment with. The two don't always agree: see call notes.
  • Reference labels were written for each call and text without looking at the model's answers; for texts, a second labeller agreed on spam-or-not 98% of the time.

All of this is one person's calls and messages. The numbers are a guide, not a benchmark: before we trust them we need data from other people too.

Which model fits on a phone

Speed on a Pixel 9 (Tensor G4), measured with Google's AI Edge Gallery: 256 tokens in, 256 out, average of 3 runs.

Gemma 3n E2BGPU3368.10.89 s
Qwen2.5 1.5B InstructGPU21913.51.24 s
  • Gemma 4 E2B is the practical choice today: the fastest, Apache-2.0 licensed (so it can be offered freely), and fast enough for a live label in about half a second and a call summary in about 10 to 15 seconds.
  • The larger E4B size was more accurate in some tasks in our tests with Gemma 3n E4B (below), but it needs about 4 GB of memory: on a 12 GB Pixel 9, loading it made Android close background apps. Gemma 4 E4B on phones with 16 GB is still to be tested.
  • The phone build of Gemma 4 E2B allows about 4,000 tokens. A typical call transcript is 1,500 to 2,000 tokens; long calls need splitting.

Screening decisions: let the model read, let rules decide

During a screened call, the assistant must make small judgements in about half a second. We compared the model with the hand-written rules of the default screening script:

TaskRulesGemma 4 E2BFinding
Is the caller asking to repeat, just saying they're there, refusing, or answering?0.60 0.93The model is much better at this.
"Is it urgent?" answered yes or no0.740.98The model is much better.
Wrong number0.99Only when the model extracts the name asked for and a fixed matcher decides. When the model decided directly, it took wrong-number callers for you.
Spam, automated or a real person?0.81Only when the model extracts (name and company, 0.92 each) and rules decide. Deciding directly, an earlier model marked 23% of real callers as spam; with extraction, 1 in 56.

The lesson so far: a small model is a good reader and a poor judge. It should fill in the caller card and classify short replies; the decision to ring you or end the call stays with rules you can read. Qwen2.5 1.5B was not good enough (0.76 on the first task; 18 of 56 real callers marked spam). The default screening script is rules-only today; these results decide what the model will be allowed to do.

Call notes (summaries)

After a call, +PLUS AI writes a short summary: the gist, then the key points with any numbers, dates and follow-ups. We compared summaries of the same calls by our models and by a commercial service's call notes.

On about 112 calls, without fine-tuningGemma 3n E2BGemma 3n E4BCommercial service
Summaries with an invented name, number or date8.9%7.1%4.5%
Wrong speaker, role or call direction26%16%
Facts covered86%86%
Better / same / worse than the commercial notes (31 calls)8 / 15 / 812 / 12 / 7

Gemma 4 E2B came out like Gemma 3n E4B (12 / 12 / 7) at E2B's size and speed. Then the phone surprised us:

  • The phone corrupts digits the PC doesn't. On the phone's GPU, the same model wrote "April 222" or "10:000 a.m.". The CPU backend on the phone was much better (calls with a wrong number: 3 of 41 against 9 on the GPU), at twice the time (about 20 s against 10 s per call).
  • Number tags fixed it. Before the model sees the transcript, every number becomes a tag ([N1], [N2]…). The model copies tags, never digits, and the numbers go back in exactly as they were said. Calls with a wrong number fell from 9 to 0 of 41, while covering about as many of the reference notes' numbers (40 of 63, against 43 before). This is in Phone+ now.
  • What didn't help: the model's built-in "thinking" (about 10 times slower and more wrong numbers), giving the transcript as JSON (more wrong numbers, fewer facts), and a second "check your numbers" pass, which helped on the PC but made things worse on the phone.
  • Still open: the summary sometimes attributes a line to the wrong person. Part of that comes from the transcript itself (echo between the two sides of a call). And the labels in our instructions ("the owner", "Other") sometimes leak into the notes. Better instructions and fine-tuning are the next experiments.

Search over past calls

Questions like "when did the dentist call about my appointment?" over a set of 40 test questions (right call first / right call in the top 5):

MethodFirstTop 5
Plain full-text search with a small date and speaker parser2734
Gemma 3n E2B choosing search calls itself1319
Gemma 4 E2B with constrained JSON and examples3033
Gemma 4 E2B with native tool calls2630

Gemma 4 E2B was the first model to beat plain search, when its answers are forced into a fixed JSON shape. Earlier models invented numbers and dates in their search requests, so plain search stays underneath as the fallback.

SMS spam detection

For a future messaging plus-up: about 1,900 texts from numbers that aren't contacts, each labelled scam, spam, promotion, transactional or personal. The models learned from 1,443 of them and were tested on the other 484 (56 spam, 145 personal), never on a template they had seen.

ClassifierSpam F1False alarmsPersonal texts flagged
2 of 4 agree (TF-IDF, DistilRoBERTa, MiniLM, fine-tuned Gemma)0.8920
2 of 3 small models agree0.8720
DistilRoBERTa, fine-tuned0.8541
TF-IDF + logistic regression (trains in 5 seconds, a few MB)0.8453
MiniLM-L12, fine-tuned (phone-sized)0.8241
Naive Bayes (classic Bayesian filter)0.801311
Gemma 4 E2B, LoRA fine-tuned0.7492
TF-IDF and Gemma both say spam0.7000
Gemma 4 E2B, no fine-tuning, on the phone0.56235
  • The user's own labels matter more than the model. Trained only on a well-known public SMS spam collection, the filter scored 0.11 with 200 false alarms; on texts invented by a model, 0.44. Today's spam doesn't look like those. A filter has to keep learning on the phone from what you mark.
  • The language model is not the spam filter by itself. Fine-tuning lifted Gemma from 0.56 to 0.74, still below a classifier that trains in seconds. But as one vote among several it helps, and when a small model and Gemma agree there were no false alarms at all: those texts could be filed away automatically, and texts only one of them flags marked "possible spam".
  • The hardest scams read like personal texts ("hi, is this…?"). They need signals beyond the words: an unknown number, no earlier conversation, a first contact.

What's next

  • Fine-tuning Gemma 4 E2B (LoRA adapters) for call notes and short labels, and comparing it with better system prompts alone.
  • Trying Gemma 4 E4B on phones with 16 GB of RAM, and the Pixel's NPU where Google's runtime supports it.
  • A spam filter that learns on the phone, with the language model as a second opinion and an explanation of why a text looks like a scam.
  • Testing everything on more people's data.

Have data to share?

The biggest limit of these experiments is that they use one person's calls and messages. If you have lots of calls or messages that are already classified (spam or not, a summary, an answer to a question) and you are willing to share them, please get in contact. We would use them only to test and train these models, keep them private, and publish only aggregate results like the ones on this page.
