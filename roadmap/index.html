Roadmap

What we are working on next, and what we are still finding out

+PLUS is an early preview. This page lists the plus-ups, features and investigations in our pipeline, grouped by how close they are. Plans change as we test on real phones; nothing here is a promise or a date.

Next up

  • Setup step for debug root. +PLUS explains the one-time Magisk step itself after the first restart with root, and choosing debug root at key import no longer starts a build until you say so.
  • GrapheneOS's setup "security preview" step. GrapheneOS's setup asks the update app for a security-preview choice; +PLUS will answer it, so setup no longer skips that step.
  • Google Phone, tested further. Hold for Me, visual voicemail, call recording where Google offers it, Play updates, and turning it off again, on more phones.
  • Phone+ fixes from testing: search results kept when you go back, labels on calls that screening turned away, and AI call notes without stray labels from the model's instructions.

Planned

Owner lock: your key after a reset or failed PINs

Make a stolen phone much less useful. Two parts, both built into the image +PLUS signs, with no change to GrapheneOS's own code:

  • After a factory reset the phone asks for your +PLUS key during setup, so a thief who resets it can't simply set it up again. GrapheneOS's setup already hands one step to the update app, which +PLUS is.
  • After a number of wrong PINs, or many hours without an unlock, the phone restarts and asks for your key before the PIN pad even appears, while your data stays encrypted. The PIN still unlocks as usual after that.

This only protects a phone with OEM unlocking turned off, and losing your key backup would then lock you out for good. It will be optional, with clear warnings, and tested on development phones first.

Several catalogs at once

Follow more than one catalog, for example ours and your own with your own apps. Each keeps its own key, serial and expiry; a catalog that is down never holds back the others or a GrapheneOS update.

More "your own apps"

Recipes for other apps that need system permissions to work properly on GrapheneOS, for example giving a call recorder you choose access to call audio. Each one is reviewed, capped by a list built into +PLUS, and opt-in.

Phone+: calling cards and company lines

Call through an access number first, then send the rest as tones, the way calling cards and office phone systems work, so a return call can show your company number. Unlimited routes, rules per number type, PINs kept encrypted and never logged.

Messaging: spam detection and texting from your other devices

A better messaging plus-up, in the same spirit as Phone+:

  • Spam detection on the phone. Classic spam filtering combined with a small language model, fine-tuned to tell scams from real messages, all running on the phone; nothing is sent anywhere to be checked. Our early results are on the AI experiments page.
  • Texting from your other devices. Read and send your SMS from your computer or tablet, with your phone as the hub, designed so your messages don't have to sit on anyone else's server.

Call screening in the car

Screen calls with Android Auto: see who is calling and why on the car's screen, and answer or decline from there.

A release signing key

The catalog is signed with a staging key during the preview. Before a real release it moves to an offline release key, and the phones' catalog link changes with it.

Investigating

  • Better on-device AI. Fine-tuning Gemma 4 for call notes, the larger E4B model on phones with more memory, and the phone's NPU.
  • Pixel 6 and Pixel 10. +PLUS supports every model GrapheneOS supports, but so far it has only been loaded onto Pixel 9 phones.

Google's on-device AI: working, but not like this

Google Phone's call notes, scam detection and "leave a message" run on Google's on-device AI: AICore with the Gemini Nano model. We have had it working on GrapheneOS. In our own source build of GrapheneOS, Google Phone's call notes produced summaries on the phone, on the Pixel's Tensor chip, from a model we supplied ourselves.

Getting there took much more than adding apps:

  • AICore and Google's Private Compute Services installed as privileged system apps;
  • the model put into AICore's storage by code we added to GrapheneOS's framework, because Google's servers refuse to send it to GrapheneOS and AICore allows no other way in;
  • more framework changes: settings that sandboxed Google Play can't set on GrapheneOS, a temporary exemption so AICore isn't frozen while it summarises after a call, and an SELinux rule;
  • for scam detection, also data Google only serves to phones that pass its integrity check, which GrapheneOS doesn't.

That is too far from GrapheneOS's security model to ship. GrapheneOS keeps Google's apps sandboxed and unprivileged, and its own code is what makes the phone secure; our build changed both. It meant our own GrapheneOS build rather than official releases, with framework patches to carry and re-check at every release, and Google components with system privileges. +PLUS exists precisely to avoid that: it uses official GrapheneOS releases unchanged and only adds apps and configuration.

We are trying to make it simpler. With +PLUS's "your own apps", AICore can already become a system app without changing GrapheneOS's code, more simply than in our source build. What is still missing is a way to put a model you obtained yourself into AICore that needs no framework change; until then these three Google Phone features stay off with +PLUS. If we find a way that keeps GrapheneOS's code untouched, it will be opt-in, with a clear warning.

Not possible yet

  • Holding the phone's encryption until your key is scanned. The owner lock can keep anyone from reaching the PIN pad, but the PIN is still what decrypts the phone; changing that needs GrapheneOS itself.
